Phase 3

Content Strategy - Tommy Bryson

Built: 2026-05-16 Frame: Content lanes that feed The Long Term Mentorship (Track A + Track B), the scorecard-driven email asset, and the activated free-call-as-application surface. The goal is to convert the 590K-subscriber YouTube + 24K IG audience into segmented email subscribers without slowing the existing YouTube cadence or diluting the "Read a Book a Week" brand pillar.

Voice anchors

  • Specific over abstract. Real numbers (Tommy's own trajectory: $1K / mo, $60 / wk, $23K / mo over 3 years). Real assets named in general terms (dividend positions, index funds, side-income lines, savings tiers). Vague does not earn trust in a niche oversaturated with vague.
  • Ten-years-in tone. The story is "the channel has been running since September 2015, here is what compounded." Plays best when stated flat. Avoid retrospective-philosophical framing.
  • Operator-credentialed, not influencer-flat. Reference the trajectory, the saved-and-protected milestones, the income-line stacking sequence. Credibility comes from the mechanics, not the views.
  • No guru-circuit vocabulary. The cliche-list lives in copy-strategy. Replace cliche language with specific outcomes - "first $1K saved-and-protected," "first dividend position opened," "first side-income line earning."
  • Investment-niche guard rails. Every content unit that touches income, dividends, side-income, investing, or passive-income outcomes includes the implicit "this is what worked for me / for this cohort grad - not personalised investment advice" framing. The explicit footer ships on every owned long-form caption.

Content pillars (four)

Pillar one - the income-line mechanics

Topic territory: the concrete work of building the first passive-income line. Income audit (where is the paycheque going). Cash-flow stabilisation (the savings rate the cohort writes against). First $1K saved-and-protected (high-yield account, separation from operating cash). First dividend or index-fund position (brokerage account opened, first deposit, first asset selected by general category - not specific stock pick). First side-income line (digital product, rental, cash-flowing skill, the three categories the cohort actually walks). The order of operations matters.

Why this pillar matters: every clip here is direct top-of-funnel for the cohort. Anyone consuming this content is either a 9-to-5 viewer wanting the first line (cohort buyer) or a curious aspiring viewer (top-of-funnel for the cohort). Zero-waste audience.

Sample hook formats:

  • "The income audit I would run on any 9-to-5 paycheque before saving a dollar."
  • "First $1K saved-and-protected. Where it goes. Why it does not stay in the checking account."
  • "First dividend position I opened. What I looked for. (Not investment advice.)"
  • "Three side-income lines the 9-to-5 worker can actually build on weekends."

Pillar two - the trajectory and the case-studies

Topic territory: the meta-content lane. The $1K / mo to $60 / wk to $23K / mo arc. The first year of zero passive income. The first paid contract from content. The first $1K month from non-9-to-5 income. Anonymous case-studies of cohort grads (post-launch) at the same beats.

Why this pillar matters: this is the cohort-pitch lane. Every clip in this pillar is teaching the audience "you can do this" via case-study evidence. The cohort productises pillar two into curriculum.

Sample hook formats:

  • "The week I made $60 and the rent was $400. What I did, what I would not do again."
  • "Year one I had zero passive income. Here is what year one actually looks like for most 9-to-5 operators."
  • "The first month I cleared $1K from a non-9-to-5 source. The exact line. The exact dollar amount."
  • "Three years from $60 / wk to $23K / mo. Honest order of operations."

Pillar three - the book-a-week reading lane

Topic territory: the brand-discipline pillar. Each week's book read with a 3-to-5-minute takeaway video. Books that compound. Books that did not. The reading framework. The connection between long-form reading and operator decisions.

Why this pillar matters: this is the brand-credibility anchor. It signals discipline, long-term thinking, and non-hype information diet to the audience. It also de-risks the cohort positioning - "Tommy reads a book a week" is a credibility signal that the cohort is taught by an operator who keeps learning, not a guru who stopped reading at the book they sell.

Sample hook formats:

  • "Book of the week. The one chapter I built a content week off."
  • "Three books that did not deliver. Read the table of contents, skipped the rest."
  • "Why I read a book a week. The compounding math of input."
  • "The book I revisited this year. What changed in the second read."

Pillar four - the net-worth-reveal and trajectory-anchor lane

Topic territory: the long-form format that pulled high-curiosity new viewers (the "Revealing My ACTUAL Net Worth" video style). Honest income breakdowns. Honest portfolio breakdowns. Honest cost-of-living breakdowns. Real numbers, real context, individual case.

Why this pillar matters: this is the entry-pillar - the audience that finds the channel for the "what does $23K / mo look like" curiosity first, learns about the cohort second. Used at scale it dilutes the operator-credentialed positioning; used at quarterly intervals it pulls top-of-funnel volume without diluting the cohort lane.

Sample hook formats:

  • "Net worth as of this quarter. Real number, real breakdown by category."
  • "What $X / mo actually looks like when most of it goes back into building."
  • "Cost of one month operating across two income lines. Real number, real assumptions."

Atomisation playbook

Every long-form piece (one YouTube long-form, one book-of-the-week read, one net-worth-reveal) is repurposable into 6-12 distinct content units. Default atomisation per long-form unit:

  • 3-5 IG reel clips (45-90 sec each), captioned, branded
  • 2-3 TikTok re-cuts
  • 1-2 YouTube Shorts pulled from the strongest 30-second cuts
  • 1-2 static IG carousels pulling the strongest framework
  • 1 long-form Facebook post pulling specific examples
  • 1 newsletter recap (post-install) pointing to the full piece
  • 1 Patreon deep-dive (post-install) on the long-form post

This turns one piece of long-form into 7-10 days of social content across four platforms without filming additional units.

Scorecard quiz - distribution plan

Once the "How Close Are You To Your First Passive Income Line?" scorecard ships, every piece of content gets a single CTA: the quiz. Not the cohort. Not the $249 budget course. Not Patreon. The quiz. The quiz captures email, scores the lead, segments by current passive-income revenue band (zero / first-line-in-build / first-line-earning / multi-line), and the email list then routes the lead toward Track A, Track B, the $249 entry course, or the free Patreon tier.

That single funnel decision - quiz first, cohort second, entry course third - is the structural unlock. Until it is built, every YouTube long-form is a free public service. After it ships, every YouTube long-form is a tracked lead-gen unit. Not investment advice. Results vary by capital, market, and operator execution.

What to stop posting

  • Generic "side hustle" listicles without an income-line operator framing
  • "Get rich quick" or "passive income on autopilot" framings (banned)
  • Specific stock picks, specific crypto positions, specific real-estate addresses (regulated-niche violations)
  • Content that hides the income mechanics behind aesthetic
  • Anything that frames the audience as if they need to be sold (the cohort audience needs to be educated and qualified, not sold)

What to start posting

  • Behind-the-scenes of an actual income audit (anonymised participant, real numbers framed in ranges)
  • Build-in-public of the cohort during the first 1-2 cycles
  • Honest income breakdowns (real numbers, real context, individual case - never extrapolated for the viewer)
  • Cohort-grad case studies once the first 1-2 cycles have completed
  • Book-of-the-week takeaway with a sharp 3-line action note

Measurement

Track three metrics weekly: (1) scorecard quiz starts, (2) quiz-to-email completion rate, (3) email-to-cohort-application rate. Everything else is secondary. The funnel either converts or does not at these three gates.

Compliance posture

Where the content touches income claims, the format is "real number, real context, individual case." No generalised "you can make $X" framing. The implicit message is "this is what worked for me / for this cohort grad" - never an extrapolated guarantee for the viewer. Every long-form caption and every cohort-related short-form caption carries the line: "Not investment advice. Results vary by capital, market, and operator execution." This is non-negotiable. The cohort is general financial education and operator coaching, not personalised investment, tax, or legal advice.
